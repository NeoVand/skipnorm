\emph{Introduction.}---Symmetry and conservation laws govern the evolution of physical systems through Noether's theorem and Liouville-Arnold integrability \cite{arnold1989mathematical}, yet incorporating exact conservation into deep neural networks remains an open challenge. In modern deep learning, normalization layers such as Layer Normalization ($\mathrm{LayerNorm}$) \cite{ba2016layer} and Root Mean Square Normalization ($\mathrm{RMSNorm}$) \cite{zhang2019root,loshchilov2025ngpt} stabilize signal propagation by projecting intermediate representations $y = f(x) \in \R^{d_{\mathrm{out}}}$ onto a manifold of constant norm $\norm{\hat{y}}_2 = \sqrt{d_{\mathrm{out}}}$. By construction, this projection annihilates the input norm $\norm{x}_2$, discarding global signal energy, contrast, or physical amplitude at every layer. Conversely, equivariant radial gates such as $\mathrm{ModReLU}$ and $SO(3)$-equivariant activations \cite{arjovsky2016unitary,weiler20183d} update the norm while freezing the unit direction $x/\norm{x}_2$:
\begin{equation}
  \underbrace{\hat{y}_{\mathrm{eq}} = g(\norm{x}_2)\frac{x}{\norm{x}_2}}_{\text{Preserves direction, maps norm}}
  \;\longleftrightarrow\;
  \underbrace{\hat{y}_{\mathrm{SN}} = \norm{x}_p \frac{f(x)}{\norm{f(x)}_p}}_{\text{Preserves norm, maps direction}}.
  \label{eq:polar_duality}
\end{equation}
As shown in Eq.~\eqref{eq:polar_duality}, the operation $\hat{y} = \norm{x}_p \, f(x)/\norm{f(x)}_p$---which we term \textbf{SkipNorm}---is the exact \emph{polar dual} of equivariant radial gating: a multiplicative skip connection that routes the $L_p$ norm $\norm{x}_p$ of the input around an arbitrary nonlinear transformation $f: \R^{d_{\mathrm{in}}} \to \R^{d_{\mathrm{out}}}$.

While linear unitary and orthogonal networks ($U^\top U = I$) preserve Euclidean norm \cite{arjovsky2016unitary,jing2017tunable,pennington2017resurrecting,anil2019sorting}, they face two fundamental obstructions: (i) by the Rank-Nullity Theorem, no linear map can be norm-preserving when compressing dimensions ($d_{\mathrm{out}} < d_{\mathrm{in}}$), and (ii) a constant unitary matrix $U$ has fixed state-independent eigenfrequencies, rendering it unable to represent nonlinear oscillators or dispersive waves whose phase velocity depends on amplitude. Meanwhile, in physics-informed machine learning, Hamiltonian Neural Networks (HNNs) \cite{greydanus2019hamiltonian}, Lagrangian networks \cite{cranmer2020lagrangian}, SympNets \cite{jin2020sympnets}, and Action-Angle networks \cite{bondesan2019learning,daigavane2022learning} require canonical symplectic coordinates $(q, p)$ where the skew-symmetric Poisson tensor $J$ is known \emph{a priori}. Furthermore, by the Ge-Marsden theorem \cite{ge1988lie}, discrete symplectic maps that preserve the phase-space 2-form $dq \wedge dp$ cannot exactly conserve a nonlinear Hamiltonian $H(q, p)$ unless they coincide with the exact time-reparameterized flow, accumulating truncation drift over long rollouts. Existing methods for automated conservation-law discovery from non-canonical trajectories---such as AI Poincar\'e \cite{liu2021machine,liu2022ai}, Siamese invariant classifiers \cite{wetzel2020discovering,ha2021discovering}, or post-hoc Hessian eigendecompositions \cite{mototake2021interpretable,iten2020discovering}---either diagnose invariant manifold dimension offline without providing a forward dynamical model, or require trajectory-segmented contrastive pairs.

In this Letter, we develop the differential geometry and algebraic topology of SkipNorm and its tangent-space extensions (\textbf{Geodesic SkipNorm} and \textbf{Toroidal Block SkipNorm}). We prove that SkipNorm acts as an exact projective radial isometry across arbitrary nonlinearities and rectangular bottlenecks, and we establish the \emph{Topological Noether-Brouwer Foliation Theorem}: a dimension-matched Geodesic SkipNorm autoencoder trained solely on trajectory prediction topologically forces its latent spheres (or tori) to foliate the unknown conservation laws of an autonomous dynamical system, simultaneously discovering non-canonical invariants and conserving them to IEEE-754 machine precision ($\sim 10^{-16}$) over arbitrary rollout horizons.

\emph{Projective radial isometry and bottleneck rank.}---Let $x \in \R^{d_{\mathrm{in}}} \setminus \{0\}$ and let $f: \R^{d_{\mathrm{in}}} \to \R^{d_{\mathrm{out}}}$ be a $C^1$ mapping such that $y = f(x) \neq 0$. For $p \in [1, \infty]$, the SkipNorm operator is defined as
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{SN}_p(x; f) := \frac{f(x)}{\max(\norm{f(x)}_p, \epsilon)} \norm{x}_p,
  \label{eq:skipnorm_def}
\end{equation}
where $\epsilon \approx 10^{-30}$ in $\mathtt{float64}$ prevents division by zero at the origin without introducing the cumulative $1/(1 + \epsilon/\norm{y}_p)$ damping artifact of additive denominators. Geometrically, under the polar diffeomorphism $\R^d \setminus \{0\} \cong \Sphere_p^{d-1} \times \R_{>0}$, SkipNorm is a fiber-bundle morphism over the radial base space $\R_{>0}$ that acts as a nonlinear map on the directional fiber $\Sphere_p^{d-1}$ while acting as the exact identity on the radial coordinate $r = \norm{x}_p$.

Our first theorem characterizes the exact Jacobian spectrum and deep gradient propagation of SkipNorm for $p=2$ (proofs of all theorems are given in the Supplemental Material).

\begin{theorem}[Orthogonal Jacobian Decomposition \& Deep Radial Isometry]
\label{thm:jacobian_isometry}
Let $\hat{y} = \mathrm{SN}_2(x; f)$ with $x \in \R^{d_{\mathrm{in}}} \setminus \{0\}$, $y = f(x) \in \R^{d_{\mathrm{out}}} \setminus \{0\}$, and unit radial vectors $u_x = x/\norm{x}_2$, $u_y = y/\norm{y}_2 = \hat{y}/\norm{\hat{y}}_2$. Let $J_f = \partial y / \partial x \in \R^{d_{\mathrm{out}} \times d_{\mathrm{in}}}$. The Jacobian $J = \partial \hat{y} / \partial x$ decomposes into two mutually orthogonal operators:
\begin{equation}
  J = \underbrace{\frac{\norm{x}_2}{\norm{y}_2} \left(I_{d_{\mathrm{out}}} - u_y u_y^\top\right) J_f}_{A \text{ (Tangential: } u_y^\top A = 0\text{)}} \;+\; \underbrace{u_y u_x^\top}_{B \text{ (Radial: rank 1)}}.
  \label{eq:jacobian_decomp}
\end{equation}
Consequently, for any composition of $L$ SkipNorm layers of arbitrary varying widths $d_0 \to d_1 \to \dots \to d_L$ with total Jacobian $J_{\mathrm{total}} = J_L J_{L-1} \cdots J_1$:
\begin{equation}
  J_{\mathrm{total}}^\top u_L \equiv u_0, \qquad \norm{J_{\mathrm{total}}^\top u_L}_2 \equiv 1.
  \label{eq:exact_radial_bprop}
\end{equation}
Moreover, if each $f_l$ is positively $1$-homogeneous ($f_l(cx) = c f_l(x)$ for $c > 0$, e.g., bias-free $\mathrm{LeakyReLU}$ or $\mathrm{ELU}$ networks), then $J_f u_x = (\norm{y}_2/\norm{x}_2) u_y \in \ker(I - u_y u_y^\top)$, so $A u_x = 0$ and $J_{\mathrm{total}}$ is a strict left- and right-singular radial isometry ($J_{\mathrm{total}} u_0 = u_L$, $\sigma_{\mathrm{radial}} = 1$).
\end{theorem}

Equation~\eqref{eq:exact_radial_bprop} reveals a striking phenomenon: even when a deep network passes through narrow bottlenecks ($d_l \ll d_0$) where linear orthogonal matrices cannot exist, the radial gradient $J_{\mathrm{total}}^\top u_L$ is transported backward across arbitrary depth $L$ with \emph{zero attenuation or amplification}, recovering the exact input direction $u_0$ [Fig.~\ref{fig:geometry_rank}(b)].

Crucially, Theorem~\ref{thm:jacobian_isometry} also explains why standard normalizations suffer catastrophic representational collapse inside low-dimensional bottlenecks (such as autoencoder latent spaces or low-rank attention heads).

\begin{theorem}[Bottleneck Rank Theorem]
\label{thm:bottleneck_rank}
Consider a representation layer of width $d \ge 2$ at any non-degenerate point $x \in \R^d \setminus \{0\}$ where $\mathrm{rank}(J_f(x)) = d$ and $J_f(x) u_x \notin \mathrm{span}(u_y)^\perp$:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{LayerNorm ($\hat{y}_{\mathrm{LN}} = \sqrt{d}\, P_{\perp \mathbf{1}} y / \norm{P_{\perp \mathbf{1}} y}_2$):} Both the mean direction $\mathbf{1}$ and the centered radial direction $u_{y_c}$ lie in the left nullspace of $J_{\mathrm{LN}}$, so $\mathrm{rank}(J_{\mathrm{LN}}) \le d - 2$. At $d = 2$, $\mathrm{rank}(J_{\mathrm{LN}}) = 0$ everywhere and the entire plane $\R^2$ collapses onto two discrete points $\{\pm(1, -1)\}$.
  \item \textbf{RMSNorm / SphericalNorm ($\hat{y}_{\mathrm{RMS}} = \sqrt{d}\, y / \norm{y}_2$):} The radial direction $u_y$ lies in the left nullspace of $J_{\mathrm{RMS}}$, so $\mathrm{rank}(J_{\mathrm{RMS}}) \le d - 1$ (collapsing $d=2$ to a $1\mathrm{D}$ circle).
  \item \textbf{SkipNorm ($\hat{y}_{\mathrm{SN}} = \norm{x}_2 \, y / \norm{y}_2$):} If $P_{\perp u_y} J_f$ has rank $d-1$ on $T_x \Sphere^{d-1}$ (satisfied generically by smooth or bi-orthant activations), then $\mathrm{rank}(J_{\mathrm{SN}}) = d$ (full rank).
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

Theorem~\ref{thm:bottleneck_rank} is verified to machine precision in Fig.~\ref{fig:geometry_rank}(a,c). At $d=2$ in $\mathtt{float64}$, the minimum singular value of $\mathrm{LayerNorm}$ is $6.4 \times 10^{-34}$ ($\mathrm{rank}=0$), $\mathrm{RMSNorm}$ is $9.9 \times 10^{-17}$ ($\mathrm{rank}=1$), and SkipNorm is $\sigma_{\min} = 0.264$ ($\mathrm{rank}=2$). Theorem~\ref{thm:jacobian_isometry} also implies an important warning regarding activation functions: placing a strict $\mathrm{ReLU}$ immediately inside SkipNorm traps outputs in the positive orthant $\Sphere_+^{d-1} = \Sphere^{d-1} \cap \R_{\ge 0}^d$; in a $d=2$ bottleneck, whenever only one neuron is active, $\hat{y}$ is pinned to a coordinate axis $(0, \norm{x}_2)$ or $(\norm{x}_2, 0)$ with zero tangential gradient. Using smooth or bi-orthant activations ($\mathrm{SiLU}$, $\mathrm{GELU}$, $\mathrm{LeakyReLU}$) preserves the full sphere $\Sphere^{d-1}$ and full Jacobian rank $d$.

\begin{theorem}[Scale Equivariance \& Weight $0$-Homogeneity]
\label{thm:homogeneity}
If $f(x; W) = \phi(Wx)$ where $\phi$ is positively $1$-homogeneous, then $\mathrm{SN}_p(cx; \beta W) = c\,\mathrm{SN}_p(x; W)$ for all $c, \beta > 0$, and $\inner{\nabla_W \mathrm{SN}_p(x; W)}{W}_F = 0$.
\end{theorem}

By Theorem~\ref{thm:homogeneity}, weight-norm drift during gradient descent has zero effect on the forward map, while out-of-distribution input scaling $x \mapsto cx$ is transmitted equivariantly with zero relative error degradation.

\emph{Tangent-space geodesic retraction and nonlinear lattice waves.}---When modeling continuous-time or recurrent dynamical systems $x_{t+1} = \Phi_{\Delta t}(x_t)$, applying naive SkipNorm $\hat{y} = \norm{x}_2 f(x)/\norm{f(x)}_2$ allows $f(x)$ to point in an arbitrary direction unrelated to $x$, while a naive additive residual $y = x + f(x)$ suffers an antipodal phase-flip singularity if $f(x) \approx -2x$. To construct a smooth near-identity flow on concentric spheres $\Sphere^{d-1}(\norm{x}_2)$, we project the residual update onto the tangent hyperplane $T_x \Sphere^{d-1} = \{v \in \R^d : \inner{v}{x} = 0\}$ prior to radial retraction, defining \textbf{Geodesic SkipNorm}:
\begin{align}
  f_\perp(x) &= f(x) - \frac{\inner{f(x)}{x}}{\max(\norm{x}_2^2, \epsilon^2)} x, \nonumber\\
  \mathrm{GSN}(x; f, \alpha) &= \frac{x + \alpha f_\perp(x)}{\sqrt{\norm{x}_2^2 + \alpha^2 \norm{f_\perp(x)}_2^2}} \norm{x}_2,
  \label{eq:geodesic_skipnorm}
\end{align}
where $\alpha \in \R$ is a learnable step scale. Because $f_\perp(x) \perp x$, the denominator $\norm{x + \alpha f_\perp(x)}_2 = \sqrt{\norm{x}_2^2 + \alpha^2 \norm{f_\perp(x)}_2^2} \ge \norm{x}_2$ is strictly bounded away from zero for all $\alpha \in \R$, and $\inner{\mathrm{GSN}(x)}{x} = \norm{x}_2^2 / \sqrt{1 + \alpha^2 \norm{f_\perp}_2^2/\norm{x}_2^2} > 0$, eliminating antipodal flips and yielding a first-order retraction onto $\Sphere^{d-1}(\norm{x}_2)$ in $\mathcal{O}(d)$ time.

To test $\mathrm{GeodesicSkipNorm}$ on a high-dimensional quantum/optical wave system where linear unitary networks fail, we consider the 1D Discrete Nonlinear Schr\"odinger (DNLS) equation on a periodic lattice of $L=16$ complex sites ($\psi \in \mathbb{C}^{16} \cong \R^{32}$):
\begin{equation}
  i \frac{d\psi_n}{dt} = -(\psi_{n+1} + \psi_{n-1} - 2\psi_n) + g \abs{\psi_n}^2 \psi_n,
  \label{eq:dnls}
\end{equation}
with Kerr nonlinearity $g = 1.5$. Equation~\eqref{eq:dnls} conserves the total wave norm (particle number) $\mathcal{N}(\psi) = \sum_{n=1}^L \abs{\psi_n}^2 = \norm{\psi}_2^2$, but its local phase rotation rate depends nonlinearly on the wave intensity $\abs{\psi_n}^2$. As shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:dnls} and Table~\ref{tab:dnls}, across 5 random seeds, a standard ResNet stepper ($\hat{\psi} = \psi + \alpha f(\psi)$) achieves low short-term error but diverges to $7.2 \times 10^{71}\%$ norm error over a $2{,}000$-step rollout. $\mathrm{RMSNorm}$ ($\norm{\hat{\psi}}_2 = \sqrt{2L}$) immediately destroys the initial wave amplitude ($1.2 \times 10^3\%$ error). A strictly linear unitary operator ($\hat{\psi} = U\psi$ parameterized via the Cayley transform $U = (I - A)(I + A)^{-1}$ with $A^\top = -A$) preserves norm but incurs $2.08\times$ higher trajectory MSE ($0.2079$ vs.\ $0.1001$) because a constant matrix $U$ cannot capture the intensity-dependent phase speed $g\abs{\psi_n}^2$. $\mathrm{GeodesicSkipNorm}$ achieves the lowest trajectory error while conserving $\mathcal{N}(\psi)$ to $7.38 \times 10^{-13}\% \pm 1.30 \times 10^{-13}\%$ over $2{,}000$ steps.

\emph{Topological Noether-Brouwer foliation of conservation laws.}---In most physical, biological, and continuum systems, conserved quantities are not the raw Euclidean norm $\norm{x}_2$; rather, they are nonlinear functions $I(x)$ (such as energy, Casimir invariants, or action integrals) defined in potentially non-canonical coordinates. Suppose we train a latent autoencoder $(\Phi_{\mathrm{enc}}, \Phi_{\mathrm{dec}})$ with $\Phi_{\mathrm{enc}}: \mathcal{M} \subset \R^N \to \R^{d_z}$ and a Geodesic SkipNorm latent stepper $g_\theta: \R^{d_z} \to \R^{d_z}$ ($\norm{g_\theta(z)}_2 \equiv \norm{z}_2$) to predict multi-step trajectories $x_{t+k} = \Phi_{\mathrm{dec}}(g_\theta^{(k)}(\Phi_{\mathrm{enc}}(x_t)))$. When is the encoder guaranteed to identify the true conservation law $I(x)$ via its latent norm $\norm{\Phi_{\mathrm{enc}}(x)}_2$?

One might naively assume that any latent dimension $d_z$ suffices. However, if $d_z > N$, the sphere $\Sphere^{d_z-1}(r_0)$ has dimension $d_z - 1 \ge N$, so an injective encoder can embed the entire $N$-dimensional phase space $\mathcal{M}$ into a \emph{single} sphere of constant radius $r_0$, producing a trivial constant norm $\norm{\Phi_{\mathrm{enc}}(x)}_2 \equiv r_0$ that fails to distinguish orbits. Our central topological theorem proves that matching the latent bottleneck dimension to the state manifold dimension ($d_z = N$) eliminates this degeneracy via Brouwer's Invariance of Domain \cite{brouwer1911beweis}.

\begin{theorem}[Topological Noether-Brouwer Foliation Theorem]
\label{thm:noether_brouwer}
Let $\mathcal{M} \subset \R^N$ ($N \ge 2$) be an open annular domain around an equilibrium $x^*$, foliated by compact, connected $(N-1)$-dimensional regular level sets $\mathcal{O}_c = \{x \in \mathcal{M} : I(x) = c\}$ for $c \in (c_{\min}, c_{\max})$ of a continuous conservation law $I: \mathcal{M} \to \R$ with $\mathcal{O}_{c_{\min}} = \{x^*\}$. Assume the dynamical flow $\Phi_{\mathrm{flow}}^t$ has a dense orbit on each $\mathcal{O}_c$. If a continuous injective encoder $\Phi_{\mathrm{enc}}: \mathcal{M} \to \R^N$ (into the \textbf{same} dimension $d_z = N$) conjugates the flow into a SkipNorm operator $g_\theta: \R^N \to \R^N$ ($\Phi_{\mathrm{enc}}(\Phi_{\mathrm{flow}}^{\Delta t}(x)) = g_\theta(\Phi_{\mathrm{enc}}(x))$), then:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Spherical Surjection:} There exists a continuous function $\psi: (c_{\min}, c_{\max}) \to \R_{>0}$ such that for every $c \in (c_{\min}, c_{\max})$, the image of the level set is the \textbf{entire} $(N-1)$-sphere:
  \begin{equation}
    \Phi_{\mathrm{enc}}(\mathcal{O}_c) = \Sphere^{N-1}(\psi(c)).
    \label{eq:spherical_surjection}
  \end{equation}
  \item \textbf{Strict Monotonicity \& Origin Alignment:} The map $\psi: (c_{\min}, c_{\max}) \to \R_{>0}$ is a strictly monotonic bijection of the conservation law $I(x)$, i.e., $\norm{\Phi_{\mathrm{enc}}(x)}_2 = \psi(I(x))$ with $\psi(c_1) \neq \psi(c_2)$ for all $c_1 \neq c_2$, and $\Phi_{\mathrm{enc}}(x^*) = 0$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof Sketch]
By conjugacy and radial isometry, $h(x) := \norm{\Phi_{\mathrm{enc}}(x)}_2$ satisfies $h(\Phi_{\mathrm{flow}}^{k\Delta t}(x)) = \norm{g_\theta^{(k)}(\Phi_{\mathrm{enc}}(x))}_2 = h(x)$. Since each orbit is dense in $\mathcal{O}_c$ and $h$ is continuous, $h$ is constant on $\mathcal{O}_c$, so $\Phi_{\mathrm{enc}}(\mathcal{O}_c) \subseteq \Sphere^{N-1}(\psi(c))$. Because $\mathcal{O}_c$ has more than one point and $\Phi_{\mathrm{enc}}$ is injective, $\psi(c) > 0$. The restriction $\Phi_{\mathrm{enc}}|_{\mathcal{O}_c}: \mathcal{O}_c \to \Sphere^{N-1}(\psi(c))$ is a continuous injection between $(N-1)$-dimensional topological manifolds without boundary. By Brouwer's Invariance of Domain Theorem, $\Phi_{\mathrm{enc}}(\mathcal{O}_c)$ is open in $\Sphere^{N-1}(\psi(c))$; since $\mathcal{O}_c$ is compact and $\Sphere^{N-1}(\psi(c))$ is Hausdorff, $\Phi_{\mathrm{enc}}(\mathcal{O}_c)$ is also closed. Since $\Sphere^{N-1}(\psi(c))$ is connected for $N \ge 2$, $\Phi_{\mathrm{enc}}(\mathcal{O}_c) = \Sphere^{N-1}(\psi(c))$. Finally, for $c_1 \neq c_2$, $\mathcal{O}_{c_1} \cap \mathcal{O}_{c_2} = \emptyset$, so injectivity of $\Phi_{\mathrm{enc}}$ forces $\Sphere^{N-1}(\psi(c_1)) \cap \Sphere^{N-1}(\psi(c_2)) = \emptyset$, yielding $\psi(c_1) \neq \psi(c_2)$. Continuity on the interval $(c_{\min}, c_{\max})$ implies strict monotonicity of $\psi$, and shrinking $\mathcal{O}_c \to \{x^*\}$ as $c \downarrow c_{\min}$ forces $\Phi_{\mathrm{enc}}(x^*) = 0$.
\end{proof}

Theorem~\ref{thm:noether_brouwer} naturally extends to integrable systems with $K$ independent commuting invariants $(I_1, \dots, I_K)$ whose joint level sets are invariant $K$-tori $\Torus^K$ \cite{arnold1989mathematical,bondesan2019learning}. Partitioning the latent space into $K$ two-dimensional blocks $z = (z^{(1)}, \dots, z^{(K)}) \in \bigoplus_{k=1}^K \R^2 \cong \R^{2K}$ and applying Geodesic SkipNorm independently to each block's norm while allowing $f(z)$ to couple all blocks nonlinearly defines \textbf{Toroidal Block SkipNorm}:
\begin{equation}
  \hat{z}^{(k)} = \mathrm{GSN}\big(z^{(k)};\, f^{(k)}(z^{(1)}, \dots, z^{(K)}),\, \alpha_k\big), \quad k = 1, \dots, K.
  \label{eq:toroidal_skipnorm}
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:toroidal_skipnorm} preserves all $K$ block radii $r_k = \norm{z^{(k)}}_2$, foliating $\R^{2K}$ into $\prod_{k=1}^K \Sphere^1(r_k) \cong \Torus^K$---constructing coordinate-free neural Liouville-Arnold action-angle variables where $r_k = \sqrt{2 J_k}$ are the conserved actions and $z^{(k)}/r_k \in \Sphere^1$ are the coupled nonlinear angles.

\emph{Discovering non-canonical and multi-action invariants.}---A critical test of Theorem~\ref{thm:noether_brouwer} is whether it holds when the true invariant $I(x)$ is completely uncorrelated with the input Euclidean norm $\norm{x}_2$. In symmetric systems centered at the origin (such as the quartic Duffing oscillator $H(q, p) = \frac{1}{2}p^2 + \frac{1}{4}q^2 + \frac{1}{2}q^4$, where $\mathrm{GeodesicSkipNorm}$ achieves $R^2 = 99.87\%$ and $\abs{\rho} = 1.000$; see Supplemental Material), outer orbits naturally have larger $\norm{(q,p)}_2$. To rule out origin-centering artifacts, we test the non-canonical Lotka-Volterra predator-prey equations in the positive quadrant $(u, v) \in \R_{>0}^2$:
\begin{equation}
  \dot{u} = \frac{2}{3}u - \frac{4}{3}uv, \qquad \dot{v} = uv - v,
  \label{eq:lotka_volterra}
\end{equation}
whose equilibrium lies at $(u^*, v^*) = (1.0, 0.5) \neq (0,0)$ and whose trajectories conserve the asymmetric logarithmic Casimir invariant $V(u, v) = u - \ln u + \frac{4}{3}v - \frac{2}{3}\ln v$. Along a single Lotka-Volterra orbit, the raw Euclidean norm $\norm{(u,v)}_2$ swings by a factor of $7$ [coefficient of variation $\mathrm{CV} = 42.33\%$; Fig.~\ref{fig:noether_foliation}(b)], yielding virtually zero Noether invariance ($R^2 = 1 - \mathbb{E}[\mathrm{Var}_t(\norm{z}_2)]/\mathrm{Var}(\norm{z}_2) = 0.09\%$, Spearman $\abs{\rho} = 0.132$).

We train autoencoders with latent steppers over a 6-step horizon ($T_{\mathrm{roll}} = 1.5\,\mathrm{s}$, sufficient to exceed the local differential-geometric Flow-Box straightening scale so orbit curvature is observed) across 5 random seeds using only trajectory prediction and latent consistency loss, with zero knowledge of $V(u,v)$ or $(u^*, v^*)$. As shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:noether_foliation}(a--c) and Table~\ref{tab:invariants}, standard ResNet ($d_z=2$) and $\mathrm{RMSNorm}$ ($d_z=2$) fail completely ($R^2 = 1.04\%$ and $0.96\%$). Crucially, overparameterized SkipNorm ($d_z = 8 > \dim\mathcal{M} = 2$) achieves small within-orbit variation ($\mathrm{CV} = 1.97\%$) by embedding the 2D plane into a nearly constant-radius shell in $\Sphere^7$, yielding poor invariant identification ($R^2 = 28.00\% \pm 21.15\%$)---directly confirming that Brouwer dimension-matching ($d_z = \dim\mathcal{M}$) is topologically essential. When dimension-matched ($d_z = 2$), $\mathrm{GeodesicSkipNorm}$ spontaneously maps the off-center equilibrium $(1.0, 0.5)$ to the latent origin $(0,0)$ and discovers the logarithmic Casimir $V(u,v)$ with $R^2 = 94.58\% \pm 1.84\%$ and Spearman rank correlation $\abs{\rho} = 0.9744 \pm 0.0077$ across all $2{,}928$ phase-space points.

\emph{Scope, limitations, and outlook.}---Three mathematical boundaries define the scope of our results: (i) because $\Sphere^{d_k-1}(r_k)$ is compact, our topological foliation applies to systems with \emph{compact} invariant level sets (periodic orbits, integrable tori, and bounded energy shells); unbounded scattering trajectories require stereographic compactification or non-compact fibers; (ii) SkipNorm guarantees exact unit radial singular value ($\sigma_r = 1$) while leaving the $d-1$ tangential singular values unconstrained, which advantageously enables nonlinear phase shear and chaotic mixing within constant-energy shells ($d \ge 3$) that linear unitary maps cannot represent; and (iii) identifying global invariants requires the training rollout horizon $H\Delta t$ to span a finite fraction of the orbit recurrence time so trajectories are not locally straightened by the differential-geometric Flow-Box lemma. By bridging projective normalization in deep learning with topological foliation in dynamical systems, SkipNorm provides a minimal, $\mathcal{O}(d)$ building block for energy-conserving neural architectures and automated scientific discovery.

\section*{S1. Proof of Theorem 1 (Orthogonal Jacobian Decomposition \& Deep Radial Isometry)}

Let $x \in \R^{d_{\mathrm{in}}} \setminus \{0\}$ and $y = f(x) \in \R^{d_{\mathrm{out}}} \setminus \{0\}$, where $f$ is continuously differentiable ($C^1$). Define the $L_2$ norms $r_x = \norm{x}_2 > 0$ and $r_y = \norm{y}_2 > 0$, and the unit radial vectors $u_x = x / r_x \in \Sphere^{d_{\mathrm{in}}-1}$ and $u_y = y / r_y \in \Sphere^{d_{\mathrm{out}}-1}$. For $\norm{y}_2 > \epsilon$, the SkipNorm map is
\begin{equation}
  \hat{y}(x) = \frac{\norm{x}_2}{\norm{y(x)}_2} y(x).
\end{equation}
Using the standard differential identities $\frac{\partial \norm{x}_2}{\partial x} = u_x^\top$ and $\frac{\partial (y / \norm{y}_2)}{\partial y} = \frac{1}{\norm{y}_2}(I_{d_{\mathrm{out}}} - u_y u_y^\top)$, the product and chain rules yield:
\begin{align}
  J(x) = \frac{\partial \hat{y}}{\partial x}
  &= \norm{x}_2 \frac{\partial (y / \norm{y}_2)}{\partial y} \frac{\partial y}{\partial x} + \frac{y}{\norm{y}_2} \frac{\partial \norm{x}_2}{\partial x} \nonumber\\
  &= \underbrace{\frac{\norm{x}_2}{\norm{y}_2} \left(I_{d_{\mathrm{out}}} - u_y u_y^\top\right) J_f(x)}_{A(x)} + \underbrace{u_y u_x^\top}_{B(x)}.
  \label{eq:supp_jac}
\end{align}
Let $P_{\perp u_y} := I_{d_{\mathrm{out}}} - u_y u_y^\top$ denote the orthogonal projection onto the tangent space $T_{u_y}\Sphere^{d_{\mathrm{out}}-1}$. Since $P_{\perp u_y}^\top u_y = P_{\perp u_y} u_y = 0$, the column space of $A(x)$ is strictly orthogonal to $u_y$, whereas the column space of $B(x) = u_y u_x^\top$ is $\mathrm{span}(u_y)$:
\begin{equation}
  A(x)^\top u_y = J_f(x)^\top P_{\perp u_y} u_y = 0, \qquad B(x)^\top u_y = (u_x u_y^\top) u_y = u_x (u_y^\top u_y) = u_x.
\end{equation}
Adding the two terms gives the exact single-layer transpose-radial identity:
\begin{equation}
  J(x)^\top u_y = A(x)^\top u_y + B(x)^\top u_y = u_x.
  \label{eq:supp_single_bprop}
\end{equation}
Now consider a composition of $L$ SkipNorm layers $h_l = \mathrm{SN}_2(h_{l-1}; f_l)$ for $l = 1, \dots, L$ with arbitrary intermediate dimensions $d_0, d_1, \dots, d_L$ and unit radial vectors $u_l = h_l / \norm{h_l}_2 \in \Sphere^{d_l-1}$. By the chain rule, $J_{\mathrm{total}} = J_L J_{L-1} \cdots J_1$. Applying Eq.~\eqref{eq:supp_single_bprop} inductively from $l = L$ down to $l = 1$:
\begin{equation}
  J_{\mathrm{total}}^\top u_L = J_1^\top J_2^\top \cdots J_{L-1}^\top (J_L^\top u_L) = J_1^\top J_2^\top \cdots J_{L-1}^\top u_{L-1} = \cdots = J_1^\top u_1 = u_0,
\end{equation}
and hence $\norm{J_{\mathrm{total}}^\top u_L}_2 = \norm{u_0}_2 = 1$, independent of the depth $L$, intermediate bottleneck widths $d_l$, or weight scales.

Finally, suppose $f(x)$ is positively $1$-homogeneous, i.e., $f(cx) = c f(x)$ for all $c > 0$. By Euler's Homogeneous Function Theorem, $J_f(x) x = f(x) = y$. Dividing by $\norm{x}_2$ gives $J_f(x) u_x = \frac{\norm{y}_2}{\norm{x}_2} u_y$. Substituting this into $A(x) u_x$ yields:
\begin{equation}
  A(x) u_x = \frac{\norm{x}_2}{\norm{y}_2} P_{\perp u_y} \left(\frac{\norm{y}_2}{\norm{x}_2} u_y\right) = P_{\perp u_y} u_y = 0, \qquad B(x) u_x = u_y (u_x^\top u_x) = u_y.
\end{equation}
Thus $J(x) u_x = u_y$ and $A(x)^\top B(x) = 0, \, A(x) B(x)^\top = 0$. Because $A(x)$ and $B(x)$ have mutually orthogonal row spaces and mutually orthogonal column spaces, the singular value decomposition (SVD) of $J(x)$ splits into the direct sum of the rank-1 radial component $u_y u_x^\top$ (with exact singular value $\sigma_{\mathrm{radial}} = 1$) and the SVD of $A(x)$ restricted to $T_{u_x}\Sphere^{d_{\mathrm{in}}-1} \to T_{u_y}\Sphere^{d_{\mathrm{out}}-1}$. $\blacksquare$

\section*{S2. Proof of Theorem 2 (Bottleneck Rank Theorem)}

Let $d \ge 2$ and let $y = f(x) \in \R^d \setminus \mathrm{span}(\mathbf{1})$ with $\mathbf{1} = (1, \dots, 1)^\top \in \R^d$.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{LayerNorm:} Let $P_{\perp \mathbf{1}} = I_d - \frac{1}{d}\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^\top$ be the centering projection ($\mathrm{rank}(P_{\perp \mathbf{1}}) = d - 1$) and $y_c = P_{\perp \mathbf{1}} y \neq 0$, with $u_{y_c} = y_c / \norm{y_c}_2$. Then $\hat{y}_{\mathrm{LN}} = \sqrt{d}\, y_c / \norm{y_c}_2$, and its Jacobian is
  \begin{equation}
    J_{\mathrm{LN}}(x) = \frac{\sqrt{d}}{\norm{y_c}_2} \left(I_d - u_{y_c} u_{y_c}^\top\right) \left(I_d - \frac{1}{d}\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^\top\right) J_f(x).
  \end{equation}
  Because $u_{y_c}^\top \mathbf{1} = 0$, the two rank-1 projections commute and their product $P_{\mathrm{LN}} = I_d - \frac{1}{d}\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^\top - u_{y_c} u_{y_c}^\top$ is the orthogonal projection onto $\mathrm{span}(\mathbf{1}, u_{y_c})^\perp$, which has dimension $d - 2$. Hence $\mathrm{rank}(J_{\mathrm{LN}}(x)) \le \mathrm{rank}(P_{\mathrm{LN}}) = d - 2$. When $d = 2$, $d - 2 = 0$, so $P_{\mathrm{LN}} = 0_{2\times 2}$ and $J_{\mathrm{LN}}(x) \equiv 0_{2\times 2}$ everywhere on $\R^2 \setminus \mathrm{span}(\mathbf{1})$, mapping the two open half-planes $y_1 > y_2$ and $y_1 < y_2$ to the two isolated points $(1, -1)$ and $(-1, 1)$.
  \item \textbf{RMSNorm:} Since $\hat{y}_{\mathrm{RMS}} = \sqrt{d}\, y / \norm{y}_2$, its Jacobian is $J_{\mathrm{RMS}}(x) = \frac{\sqrt{d}}{\norm{y}_2} P_{\perp u_y} J_f(x)$. Since $\mathrm{rank}(P_{\perp u_y}) = d - 1$, $\mathrm{rank}(J_{\mathrm{RMS}}(x)) \le d - 1$.
  \item \textbf{SkipNorm:} From Eq.~\eqref{eq:supp_jac}, $J_{\mathrm{SN}}(x) = A(x) + u_y u_x^\top$, where $\mathrm{im}(A(x)) \subseteq \mathrm{span}(u_y)^\perp$. Since $u_y \perp \mathrm{im}(A(x))$ and $u_y^\top J_{\mathrm{SN}}(x) = u_x^\top \neq 0$, $\mathrm{rank}(J_{\mathrm{SN}}(x)) = \mathrm{rank}(A(x)) + 1$. When $P_{\perp u_y} J_f(x)$ has rank $d - 1$, $\mathrm{rank}(J_{\mathrm{SN}}(x)) = (d - 1) + 1 = d$. $\blacksquare$
\end{enumerate}

\section*{S3. Proof of Theorem 3 (Scale Equivariance \& Weight $0$-Homogeneity)}

Since $f(cx; \beta W) = \phi(\beta c W x) = \beta c \, f(x; W)$ for $\beta, c > 0$, we have $\norm{f(cx; \beta W)}_p = \beta c \norm{f(x; W)}_p$ and $\norm{cx}_p = c \norm{x}_p$. Substituting into Eq.~\eqref{eq:skipnorm_def} cancels $\beta$ and factors out $c$:
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{SN}_p(cx; \beta W) = \frac{\beta c \, f(x; W)}{\beta c \norm{f(x; W)}_p} (c \norm{x}_p) = c \, \mathrm{SN}_p(x; W).
\end{equation}
Differentiating $\mathrm{SN}_p(x; \beta W) = \mathrm{SN}_p(x; W)$ with respect to $\beta$ at $\beta = 1$ gives $\inner{\nabla_W \mathrm{SN}_p(x; W)}{W}_F = 0$. By induction over $L$ composed layers, a deep bias-free SkipNorm network satisfies $F(cx; \{\beta_l W_l\}) = c F(x; \{W_l\})$. $\blacksquare$

\section*{S4. Complete Proof of Theorem 4 (Topological Noether-Brouwer Foliation Theorem)}

Let $\mathcal{M} \subset \R^N$ ($N \ge 2$) be an open domain homeomorphic to $\Sphere^{N-1} \times (c_{\min}, c_{\max}) \cup \{x^*\}$, foliated by compact, connected $(N-1)$-dimensional topological manifolds $\mathcal{O}_c = I^{-1}(c)$ for $c \in (c_{\min}, c_{\max})$ and $\mathcal{O}_{c_{\min}} = \{x^*\}$. Let $\Phi_{\mathrm{enc}}: \mathcal{M} \to \R^N$ be a continuous injective map (guaranteed whenever a continuous decoder $\Phi_{\mathrm{dec}}: \R^N \to \R^N$ satisfies $\Phi_{\mathrm{dec}}(\Phi_{\mathrm{enc}}(x)) = x$ on $\mathcal{M}$), and let $g_\theta: \R^N \to \R^N$ satisfy $\norm{g_\theta(z)}_2 = \norm{z}_2$ for all $z \in \R^N$ and $\Phi_{\mathrm{enc}}(\Phi_{\mathrm{flow}}^{\Delta t}(x)) = g_\theta(\Phi_{\mathrm{enc}}(x))$ for all $x \in \mathcal{M}$.

\textbf{Step 1 (Orbital invariance of the latent norm):} Define $h: \mathcal{M} \to \R_{\ge 0}$ by $h(x) = \norm{\Phi_{\mathrm{enc}}(x)}_2$. By induction on $k \in \mathbb{N}$,
\begin{equation}
  h(\Phi_{\mathrm{flow}}^{k\Delta t}(x)) = \norm{\Phi_{\mathrm{enc}}(\Phi_{\mathrm{flow}}^{k\Delta t}(x))}_2 = \norm{g_\theta^{(k)}(\Phi_{\mathrm{enc}}(x))}_2 = \norm{\Phi_{\mathrm{enc}}(x)}_2 = h(x).
\end{equation}
Since the discrete-time orbit $\{\Phi_{\mathrm{flow}}^{k\Delta t}(x_0)\}_{k \in \mathbb{N}}$ is dense in $\mathcal{O}_c$ (topological transitivity) and $h$ is continuous on $\mathcal{M}$, $h$ is constant on the closure $\overline{\{\Phi_{\mathrm{flow}}^{k\Delta t}(x_0)\}} = \mathcal{O}_c$. Thus there exists a well-defined function $\psi: [c_{\min}, c_{\max}) \to \R_{\ge 0}$ such that $h(x) = \psi(c)$ for all $x \in \mathcal{O}_c$, i.e., $\Phi_{\mathrm{enc}}(\mathcal{O}_c) \subseteq \Sphere^{N-1}(\psi(c))$. Since $\mathcal{M}$ has a continuous section $s: [c_{\min}, c_{\max}) \to \mathcal{M}$ with $I(s(c)) = c$, $\psi(c) = h(s(c))$ is continuous.

\textbf{Step 2 (Strict positivity $\psi(c) > 0$ on regular level sets):} For any $c \in (c_{\min}, c_{\max})$, $\mathcal{O}_c$ is a connected $(N-1)$-manifold with $N \ge 2$, so $\mathcal{O}_c$ contains infinitely many distinct points $x_1 \neq x_2$. If $\psi(c) = 0$, then $\Phi_{\mathrm{enc}}(\mathcal{O}_c) \subseteq \Sphere^{N-1}(0) = \{0\}$, which implies $\Phi_{\mathrm{enc}}(x_1) = \Phi_{\mathrm{enc}}(x_2) = 0$, contradicting the injectivity of $\Phi_{\mathrm{enc}}$. Hence $\psi(c) > 0$ for all $c \in (c_{\min}, c_{\max})$.

\textbf{Step 3 (Brouwer Invariance of Domain \& Clopen Surjection):} Fix $c \in (c_{\min}, c_{\max})$. Since $\psi(c) > 0$, $\Sphere^{N-1}(\psi(c))$ is a connected, Hausdorff, $(N-1)$-dimensional topological manifold without boundary. Consider the restricted map
\begin{equation}
  \widetilde{\Phi}_c := \Phi_{\mathrm{enc}}\big|_{\mathcal{O}_c} : \mathcal{O}_c \longrightarrow \Sphere^{N-1}(\psi(c)).
\end{equation}
Because $\Phi_{\mathrm{enc}}$ is continuous and injective on $\mathcal{M}$, $\widetilde{\Phi}_c$ is a continuous injective map from an $(N-1)$-dimensional manifold without boundary into an $(N-1)$-dimensional manifold without boundary. By \textbf{Brouwer's Invariance of Domain Theorem} \cite{brouwer1911beweis} applied in local $(N-1)$-dimensional coordinate charts, $\widetilde{\Phi}_c$ is an open map, so its image $U_c := \Phi_{\mathrm{enc}}(\mathcal{O}_c)$ is an \emph{open} subset of $\Sphere^{N-1}(\psi(c))$. Simultaneously, since $\mathcal{O}_c$ is compact and $\widetilde{\Phi}_c$ is continuous, $U_c$ is compact; since $\Sphere^{N-1}(\psi(c))$ is Hausdorff, $U_c$ is a \emph{closed} subset of $\Sphere^{N-1}(\psi(c))$. Because $U_c$ is non-empty and both open and closed in the connected space $\Sphere^{N-1}(\psi(c))$ ($N \ge 2$), we must have $U_c = \Sphere^{N-1}(\psi(c))$, establishing Eq.~\eqref{eq:spherical_surjection}.

\textbf{Step 4 (Strict monotonicity of $\psi$ and equilibrium alignment):} Suppose $c_1 \neq c_2$ in $(c_{\min}, c_{\max})$. Since $I: \mathcal{M} \to \R$ is single-valued, $\mathcal{O}_{c_1} \cap \mathcal{O}_{c_2} = \emptyset$. By global injectivity of $\Phi_{\mathrm{enc}}$, their images are disjoint:
\begin{equation}
  \emptyset = \Phi_{\mathrm{enc}}(\mathcal{O}_{c_1}) \cap \Phi_{\mathrm{enc}}(\mathcal{O}_{c_2}) = \Sphere^{N-1}(\psi(c_1)) \cap \Sphere^{N-1}(\psi(c_2)),
\end{equation}
which immediately implies $\psi(c_1) \neq \psi(c_2)$. Thus $\psi: (c_{\min}, c_{\max}) \to \R_{>0}$ is a continuous injective real-valued function on an interval, hence \emph{strictly monotonic}. Finally, as $c \downarrow c_{\min}$, $\mathrm{diam}(\mathcal{O}_c) \to 0$ with $\mathcal{O}_c \to \{x^*\}$, so $\mathrm{diam}(\Sphere^{N-1}(\psi(c))) = 2\psi(c) \to 0$ by continuity of $\Phi_{\mathrm{enc}}$, proving $\lim_{c \downarrow c_{\min}} \psi(c) = 0$ and $\Phi_{\mathrm{enc}}(x^*) = 0$. $\blacksquare$
